Frequently asked questions

Straight from the game's own FAQ.

How do I know I'm not on a fake or phishing copy of this site?

Check two things every time you play: (1) the contract address this page loads must match the canonical address the operator publishes outside of this page — their official announcement channel, GitHub, etc. — since a fake copy of this page could show a fake address too; and (2) the domain in your browser's address bar must be listed by the contract's own getMirrors() function, which only the contract's owner can update. Both checks rely on data read directly from the blockchain, not on anything the page claims about itself, so even a byte-for-byte copy hosted elsewhere can't pass either check. If a link sends you to a domain that isn't on that on-chain list, treat it as a scam and don't connect your wallet.

Can the contract, or the site operator, see my cards?

No. The deck is shuffled and masked by the players themselves — client-side, in each player's own browser — before it ever reaches the contract. Cards are elliptic-curve points; the contract only stores masked values, verifies zero-knowledge proofs, and learns a card only when it is a community card the whole table has opened. As long as one player's shuffle is honest, no other player and no group of other players can learn your cards.

What's the rake?

A fixed 3%, uncapped per pot, taken only from pots actually won at fold, showdown, or forfeit — never from a voided hand where blinds are simply returned. It's owner-adjustable but hard-capped at 5% by the contract itself, the same rate for every table at every stake at any given moment, and a rate change only ever applies to pots raked after that change, never retroactively.

Where's the gaming license and RNG certificate?

There isn't one, because there's no licensable business here to certify: no company, no site, no server, and no in-house operator running this game on anyone's behalf. What you're interacting with is a smart contract — a public utility deployed on the blockchain — that runs autonomously exactly as written, the same way for every player, forever. There's no RNG to certify in the traditional sense either: cards are shuffled and encrypted jointly by the players themselves, independently verifiable by anyone via "Verify This Hand." None of this makes real-money poker legal everywhere — gambling law is set by your jurisdiction, and it's on you to check and follow the law wherever you are before you play.

Why Polygon?

This runs on the Polygon Blockchain (Chain ID 137), a proof-of-stake network that's Ethereum-compatible but built for exactly this kind of use case: low gas fees (a tiny fraction of a cent per action), fast confirmations (a couple of seconds), full EVM smart contract support, strong security backed by its own validator set with periodic Ethereum checkpoints, and wide wallet and exchange support. Together, that's what makes a fully on-chain, dealerless poker game actually playable.

What if I lose a hand because of a software bug?

We build and test in good faith, checking the code rigorously before every release, so this shouldn't happen — but if it does, that loss is yours to bear, not the site's or its operators'. This is software running real money on a public blockchain with no support desk that can reverse a transaction once it's confirmed, so only play at stakes you'd genuinely accept losing outright to a bug or a missed timeout. Keep a few extra POL in your wallet for gas, separate from your USDT buy-in, and play on a stable connection — every betting turn has a hard clock, and a missed one auto-folds your hand regardless of the reason.

How do I actually run this? I've never used crypto before.

Three things, set up once. A browser: any recent Chrome, Brave, Edge, or Firefox — avoid in-app browsers, which often block wallet connections. A wallet: install MetaMask (metamask.io), a free app that holds your funds and signs actions on your behalf — on mobile, use WalletConnect instead to link a wallet app on your phone. The right network and currencies: this runs on Polygon, so you'll need POL to pay small network fees for every action, and USDT, the stablecoin your buy-in is denominated in — both bought on Polygon from any major exchange or inside MetaMask itself. Without POL you can't act at the table, including folding; without USDT you can't buy chips at all.
